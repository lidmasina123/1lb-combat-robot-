 Drive System

After considering the power available from Blitz Lite motors, I concluded that traction would likely become the limiting factor. Many hammersaw robots use four-wheel drive to improve traction and control, and the Blitz Lite motors provide more power than a typical 1 lb robot requires. They are also relatively expensive for my budget.

Because of this, I started looking for alternatives that could provide strong drive performance while remaining lightweight and making a 4WD setup easy to implement.

After extensive research, I chose the Repeat Robotics Tangent Drive Motor. It offers approximately 15% more stator volume than the Blitz Lite while weighing less than one-third as much. Since it does not use a gearbox and instead relies on wheel diameter for reduction, it is both lighter and mechanically simpler.

Chassis
<img width="763" height="672" alt="Screenshot 2026-06-25 135015" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5d534b79-aab8-4710-bf06-bdb490ae1781" />

I decided to make the robot wider and shorter. This should make it easier to control opponents and reduce the number of approach angles where I am at a disadvantage.

The robot is now primarily constructed from TPU for the front and armor sections, while the drive pods are made from PA12-CF.

I chose PA12-CF for the drive pods because they need to be stiff and rigid. A softer material could flex under load, causing drivetrain binding or reduced efficiency.
